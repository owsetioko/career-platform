Privacy

What this site collects when you visit, why, who receives it, and what you can do about it.

Last updated [TO CONFIRM: date Google Analytics goes live].

In short

  • There are no accounts, sign-up forms or contact forms for visitors.
  • If you allow it, the site uses Google Analytics to count page views and clicks on links to other websites. Google Analytics stores cookies in your browser.
  • Like almost every website, the hosting service keeps basic logs of each request.
  • I don't sell this information, and I don't use it for advertising.
  • Google Analytics runs only after you choose Accept in the cookie banner. If you decline, or your browser doesn't run JavaScript, nothing is sent to Google. See Your choices.

What's collected

Google Analytics

Only if you accept, each time a page loads a script from Google runs in your browser and sends Google:

  • the address and title of the page, and the page you came from if your browser shares it
  • basic device and browser details, such as browser type, operating system, screen size and language
  • roughly how long the page was open and in front of you
  • your approximate location (country, region or city). Google works this out from your IP address. Google says Google Analytics 4 doesn't log or store IP addresses.
  • a random ID stored in a cookie, so that repeat visits from the same browser count as the same visitor. The ID is not linked to your name.
  • when you click a link to another website, such as GitHub, the address of that link

I don't send Google your name, email address or anything else that identifies you, and the site has no forms for you to type into. The tag turns off Google signals, which can connect visits to people signed in to a Google account, and turns off advertising personalization.

Cookies

  • _ga and _ga_X0HGGMK1T8: set by Google Analytics on this site's domain, only after you accept, to tell visitors apart. By default they last up to two years, and each visit extends them. Choosing Decline later deletes them.
  • analytics-consent: not a cookie but an entry in your browser's local storage that remembers whether you chose Accept or Decline. It is never sent anywhere.
  • resume_session: used only by the owner's sign-in area at /admin, and only when that area is used. The public pages don't set it.

Hosting logs

The site runs on Railway. For each request, the app's log records your IP address, the time, the page you asked for and whether it loaded. Railway's own network may record further details, such as your browser's identifying string.

Email

If you email me, the message is stored by Zoho Mail, which hosts my mailbox. I use it only to reply to you.

Why

  • Analytics: to see how many people visit, where they arrive from, and which outside links get clicked, so I can tell whether the site is helping my job search.
  • Hosting logs: to keep the site running, fix errors and spot abuse.

I don't use this information to build profiles of individual people, and I don't sell it.

  • Analytics cookies: up to two years in your browser, unless you decline or delete them sooner.
  • Your consent choice: in your browser until you clear this site's data.

  • Email: until I delete it.

Your choices

  • Accept or Decline in the banner shown on your first visit. Nothing goes to Google until you accept.
  • Change your mind at any time with "Cookie settings" at the bottom of each page. Choosing Decline stops Analytics and deletes its cookies.
  • Block or delete cookies in your browser settings, or use a private window. Deleting the _ga cookies resets your random ID.
  • Use a browser setting or extension that blocks trackers. Many of them block Google Analytics.
  • Install Google's Google Analytics Opt-out Browser Add-on for desktop browsers.

If your browser blocks local storage, the site can't remember your choice, so the banner appears again on each page. The site doesn't read Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signals; use the banner instead. Hosting logs are recorded for every request, and you can't opt out of them.

Analytics records are tied to a random cookie ID, not to your name, so I usually can't find or delete one person's records. If you have a question or request, email [TO CONFIRM: contact address] and I'll do what I can.

Changes

If what the site collects changes, for example if I add a tool, I'll update this page and the date at the top.
